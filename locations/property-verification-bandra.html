What to pay extra attention to in Bandra

In Bandra the distinctive thing to confirm is ownership versus tenancy — whether a flat is held on full ownership or on a tenanted (pagdi) basis changes your rights entirely — along with the society’s conveyance status, the occupancy certificate and any redevelopment position. At the premium end, price benchmarking against actual registered sales matters: headline asking prices and real transacted prices can diverge widely.

  • Ownership vs tenancy (pagdi). Whether the flat is full ownership or tenanted, and exactly what rights transfer to you.
  • Title chain & encumbrance. Ownership traced back and the registered charges pulled, so no dispute surfaces after possession.
  • MahaRERA status. Registration, revised completion dates and complaints for the specific project and phase.
  • Builder track record. Delivery history, litigation and financial-stress signals for the developer.
  • Price benchmarking. The asking price against registered comparable sales in the same micro-market.
  • Total cost of ownership. Stamp duty, registration, GST and every builder-side charge, totalled upfront.

Bandra questions

What should I check before buying a flat in Bandra?

First, whether it is a full-ownership flat or tenanted (pagdi) stock, since that changes your rights entirely. Then the society’s conveyance status, the occupancy certificate, the title chain, and the price against registered comparable sales. We cover all of these.

What is the difference between an ownership flat and a pagdi flat?

In an ownership flat you own the flat and a share in the society. In a pagdi (tenanted) arrangement you hold tenancy rights, not outright ownership, with different transfer and resale conditions. We confirm which one a Bandra property actually is before you commit.
